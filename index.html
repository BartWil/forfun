      That body is a biotensegrity model. The white members are bones: compression struts that never touch each other. Every teal line is a tension member, standing in for muscle, tendon and fascia, and it can only pull, never push, because tissue cannot push a rope. The tension net is continuous, so load applied anywhere travels everywhere. Drag one shoulder sideways and the opposite knee follows just as far. Drag a knee and the head swings further than your hand does. Those long diagonals running from each shoulder to the opposite pelvis have a name: the posterior oblique sling, latissimus to thoracolumbar fascia to opposite gluteus maximus. Now try a hand. Almost nothing else moves, because an arm pivots freely at the shoulder and hangs off the network from outside it. That is one reason shoulder girdle problems behave so differently from spinal ones.

      Solved each frame by Verlet integration and constraint relaxation. Worth admitting: struts and one-way cables on their own cannot restore a shape. Once deformed, the rig will happily sit in whatever slack configuration it lands in. So the solver also computes the best-fit rigid motion of the rest pose each frame and eases the body back toward it, standing in for the elastic recoil that prestressed fascia provides in a real body. Shape recovery after release is exact. The motes drifting behind the body are true Newtonian orbits from 780 bytes of hand-assembled WebAssembly, embedded in the page so there is nothing to download and nothing that can fail.
