      A conceptual mechanical toy, not a validated model of human mechanics. Biotensegrity is a way of thinking about how bodies might distribute load, and it remains a theoretical proposal under discussion rather than established whole-body mechanics. Everything below describes this rig. Where it behaves like a person, enjoy the coincidence and check it against a real measurement before believing it.

      That body is a biotensegrity model. The white members are bones: compression struts that never touch each other. Every teal line is a tension member, standing in for muscle, tendon and fascia, and it can only pull, never push, because tissue cannot push a rope. Because this net is continuous, load applied anywhere in the model travels everywhere in it. Drag one shoulder sideways and the opposite knee follows just as far. Drag a knee and the head swings further than your hand does. The long diagonals running from each shoulder to the opposite pelvis are placed where anatomy has a real structure, the posterior oblique sling, running latissimus to thoracolumbar fascia to opposite gluteus maximus. That the members are anatomically placed does not make the rig's behaviour a measurement of yours. Now try a hand. Almost nothing else moves, because in this rig an arm pivots freely at the shoulder and hangs off the network from outside it.

      Solved each frame by Verlet integration and constraint relaxation. Worth admitting: struts and one-way cables on their own cannot restore a shape. Once deformed, the rig will happily sit in whatever slack configuration it lands in. So the solver also computes the best-fit rigid motion of the rest pose each frame and eases the body back toward it, standing in for the elastic recoil that prestressed fascia provides in a real body. Shape recovery after release is exact. The motes drifting behind the body are true Newtonian orbits from 780 bytes of hand-assembled WebAssembly, embedded in the page so there is nothing to download and nothing that can fail.
