What you're looking at

The motion is measured, not animated by hand

The hip, knee, and ankle rotations come from the Fukuchi et al. 2018 open walking data set — 3D motion capture of 42 healthy adults walking overground at a comfortable speed. We took the grand-average sagittal joint angles (averaged over every subject and both legs, 84 gait curves) and retargeted them straight onto the skeleton. Nothing here is a canned game animation.

Swing ~62–100% foot in the air

The timeline is one gait cycle — one foot's ground contact to that same foot's next contact — rescaled to 0–100%. In walking the foot is down ~62% of the cycle (there's no flight phase, unlike running). The other leg runs exactly half a cycle behind.

How a body walks from angles alone

Joint angles say how the limbs bend but not where the body is in space — that pelvis motion isn't in the angle data. So the walking is reconstructed with foot-locking: the foot in contact is pinned to the ground and the body is carried over it. The camera rides along, so the figure looks in place while the ground slides past and the feet stay genuinely planted.

Where that reconstruction falls short — and why it's worth knowing. Foot-locking gets you about 0.8 m of travel per gait cycle, but a real adult walking at this speed covers roughly 1.4 m per cycle. The missing half comes from everything that isn't a sagittal hip, knee or ankle angle: pelvic rotation in the transverse plane, pelvic tilt and list, and the way the foot rolls from heel to toe rather than pivoting about a point. Sagittal angles alone can never reproduce stride length — which is exactly why gait labs measure the pelvis too. Treat the forward speed here as illustrative; the joint angles are the measured part.

The body itself is a generic rigged mannequin (three.js “Xbot”). The data driving it is real; the mesh is just the canvas. Sagittal plane only. Note too that the 42-subject average pools 24 young and 18 older adults, so it is a composite of two age groups rather than a picture of any one walker.

Data: Fukuchi CA, Fukuchi RK, Duarte M (2018), A public data set of overground and treadmill walking kinematics and kinetics of healthy individuals, PeerJ 6:e4640 / figshare 5722711. Rendering: three.js. Educational visualisation — not a clinical or individual assessment.
