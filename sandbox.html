Gait Lab

Healthy walking is a chain of finely-timed events — and when one link weakens, the body improvises. Switch on a deficit and watch how the walk changes: the ghost is typical gait, the solid figure is the same person coping with the problem. The classic compensations here are exactly what clinicians look for in a gait exam.

A teaching abstraction in the sagittal (side-on) plane: the primary deficit and its main compensations are modelled on the walking kinematics. One major pattern is missing by construction: the Trendelenburg gait of weak hip abductors — where the pelvis drops on the swing side, or the trunk lurches over the stance hip to spare them — happens in the frontal plane and simply cannot be drawn from a side-on view. It is arguably the deviation physiotherapy students are asked about most, so see it in 3-D on the Knee Control page, where pelvic drop is modelled directly. The same applies to circumduction. Grounded in Perry & Burnfield, Gait Analysis: Normal and Pathological Function (2nd ed.). Not a diagnostic tool.
