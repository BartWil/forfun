The idea

One cycle, three signals

↕

Ground reaction force

The force the ground pushes back on your body with, in multiples of body weight (BW). Every push you make, the ground makes right back.

⟲

Joint angles

How much the hip, knee, and ankle flex and extend through the movement cycle — the kinematic signature of each task.

⚡

Muscle activation

Which muscle groups switch on and off, and how hard, to control and power the movement — based on classic EMG findings.

Walking and running are measured data — real forces, angles and EMG from open datasets of healthy adults. The jump and squat are reconstructions from the classic literature. Every curve is a group average (see Sources): built to teach the pattern, not to describe any individual.

Grounded in research

Sources

Walking and running are driven by real measured data. The ground reaction forces and joint angles are grand averages from the Fukuchi open data sets; the muscle activation is computed from the raw EMG of the Santuz open data sets (25,000+ gait cycles). The countermovement jump and squat are illustrative reconstructions informed by the classic literature below — the source badge on each chart tells you which you are looking at.

Measured data driving this site

  • Fukuchi CA, Fukuchi RK, Duarte M. A public dataset of overground and treadmill walking kinematics and kinetics in healthy individuals. PeerJ 6:e4640, 2018. paper · data — walking vertical GRF and sagittal hip/knee/ankle angles, grand average of 42 volunteers (24 young, 18 older adults), at three overground speeds.
  • Fukuchi RK, Fukuchi CA, Duarte M. A public dataset of running biomechanics and the effects of running speed on lower extremity kinematics and kinetics. PeerJ 5:e3298, 2017. paper · data — running GRF and joint angles, 39 runners at 2.5/3.5/4.5 m/s.
  • Santuz A, Ekizos A, Janshen L, Baltzopoulos V, Arampatzis A. Modular control of human movement during running: an open access data set. Front. Physiol., 2018. Zenodo data set — running muscle activation: grand-average EMG envelopes computed here from the raw signals (11,388 cycles, 135 adults).
  • Santuz A, Janshen L, Brüll L, Munoz-Martel V, Taborri J, Rossi S, Arampatzis A. Sex-specific tuning of modular muscle activation patterns for locomotion in young and older adults. 2021. Zenodo data set — walking muscle activation, computed here from the raw signals (13,693 cycles, 140 adults).

Texts and papers behind the reconstructions and explanations

  • Perry J, Burnfield JM. Gait Analysis: Normal and Pathological Function, 2nd ed. SLACK Incorporated, 2010. — gait phases, the three rockers, and the pathological gait patterns in the Gait Lab.
  • Novacheck TF. The biomechanics of running. Gait & Posture, 1998. — running phase structure.
  • Winter DA. Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement, 4th ed. Wiley, 2009.
  • Neumann DA. Kinesiology of the Musculoskeletal System: Foundations for Rehabilitation, 3rd ed. Elsevier, 2017. — lever systems, moment arms, length–tension.
  • Nordin M, Frankel VH. Basic Biomechanics of the Musculoskeletal System, 4th ed. Wolters Kluwer, 2012.
  • Bobbert MF, van Ingen Schenau GJ. Coordination in vertical jumping. J Biomech, 1988. — proximal-to-distal sequencing in the countermovement jump.
  • Escamilla RF. Knee biomechanics of the dynamic squat exercise. Med Sci Sports Exerc, 2001.
  • McErlain-Naylor S, King M, Pain MTG. Determinants of countermovement jump performance: a kinetic and kinematic analysis. J Sports Sci, 2014.
  • Decker MJ, Torry MR, Wyland DJ, Sterett WI, Steadman JR. Gender differences in lower extremity kinematics, kinetics and energy absorption during landing. Clin Biomech, 2003.
  • Murray WM, Buchanan TS, Delp SL. The isometric functional capacity of muscles that cross the elbow. J Biomech 33:943–952, 2000. — measured elbow moment arms and muscle architecture used on the Levers page.
  • Hill AV. The heat of shortening and the dynamic constants of muscle. Proc R Soc Lond B, 1938. — the force–velocity relationship behind the Muscle Dyno.
  • Nachemson A. The effect of forward leaning on lumbar intradiscal pressure. Acta Orthop Scand 35:314–328, 1965.
  • Wilke HJ, Neef P, Caimi M, Hoogland T, Claes LE. New in vivo measurements of pressures in the intervertebral disc in daily life. Spine 24(8):755–762, 1999.
