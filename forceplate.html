    Six channels, eight decisions

    Force Plate Lab

    A force plate does not measure a jump. It measures three forces and three moments, a thousand times a second, and every number a coach or a clinician ever quotes from it is something an analyst decided to compute. Here you make those decisions yourself, in order, and watch the answer move when you change your mind.

    This recording is synthetic. It is a teaching model, not a measurement of a person, and it says so on every screen.

    Why the data are made up, and why that is the honest choice here. A centre of pressure cannot be computed from three forces alone. It needs the plate moments as well, and a stated coordinate system, and no open dataset we could find publishes all six channels together with an unambiguous convention. A recording that pretended to be a measurement would be worse than an admitted model on a page whose entire subject is where numbers come from. So this signal is generated, and generated the hard way: an acceleration of the centre of mass is integrated into a velocity and a height, and the vertical force follows from Newton's second law. Nothing is drawn to look convincing. That is why the impulse you compute below and the flight time you measure agree with each other, and it is the only reason they can.

    Zero comes before everything

    The amplifier in this recording has an offset of a few newtons, which is ordinary and which every plate has. If you never remove it, every force you report afterwards is wrong by that constant, and the error hides inside results that otherwise look completely reasonable. The window you must average is the one where nobody is on the plate, not any quiet-looking stretch. The first version of this station had no unloaded period at all, and the check caught it immediately: a student would have subtracted 693.70 N instead of 7 N and zeroed away their own body weight along with the offset.

    Where the foot leaves the plate is your decision

    Nothing in the file says "take-off happened here". You pick a force below which you declare the foot to be off the plate, and that choice is an assumption you are making about the measurement, not a fact you are reading from it. A toe does not release in one sample: the force falls quickly and then lingers, so a low threshold declares take-off later than a high one. In this recording moving from 5 N to 50 N changes the computed jump height from 26.6 cm to 27.4 cm. Eight millimetres, from nothing but the number you typed.

    Two roads to the same height

    The impulse route adds up the force above body weight over time and converts it to a take-off velocity, because impulse equals change of momentum. The flight-time route ignores the force entirely and uses only how long the person was in the air. They come from different parts of the signal and they land within half a centimetre of each other. That agreement is not decoration: it is the check that tells you the processing was done correctly, and it is the first thing worth computing when a plate gives you a number you were not expecting.

    When the centre of pressure stops existing

    The centre of pressure is a quotient, and its denominator is the vertical force. While the person stands on the plate it sits sensibly under the feet, wandering a centimetre or two as they sway. During the flight phase the denominator goes to zero and the quotient goes wherever the noise sends it, reporting positions metres outside a plate that is only half a metre across. The person did not move. There is simply no centre of pressure without meaningful contact, and any software that keeps drawing one is drawing an artefact. Scrub the time slider into the flight phase and watch it happen.

    The last question

    Suppose the peak vertical force in a landing rises from 2.1 to 2.5 times body weight between two sessions. Did the load on the knee joint rise by the same proportion? It is tempting, it is what the number seems to say, and it does not follow. The plate measures what passes between the whole body and the ground. What passes through one joint depends on how the limb was arranged and how the muscles shared the work, and neither of those is in this file. Cannot be determined is the correct answer, and being able to give it with confidence is most of what this station is for.
